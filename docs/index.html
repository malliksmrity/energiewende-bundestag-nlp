In the Bundestag, nuclear power faded from energy speeches while hydrogen and climate protection grew

In a sample of 1,161 energy speeches, about 32 percent mentioned nuclear terms in 2009 to 2013. By 2017 to 2021 it was 10 percent. Hydrogen went from 2.5 to 14 percent.

Data: GermaParl, plenary protocols of the German Bundestag (PolMine project), periods 17 to 19. Every 8th session, speeches that mention an energy keyword.

Try it live: paste German text and see the energy terms found by the spaCy pipeline and a (weak) guess of the party. It runs in your browser.

What the numbers show

Are the changes real?

Speeches from the same session are not independent, so whole sessions were resampled 1,000 times to get a 95 percent range for the change from period 17 to period 19, in percentage points.

How it works

  1. Download every 8th Bundestag session of three legislative periods and keep speeches that mention an energy word.
  2. Clean the German text (lemmas, stop words) and look for energy terms such as Atomkraft or Wasserstoff.
  3. Group speeches into topics with two methods (LDA and BERTopic) and compare them.
  4. As an extra, predict the speaker’s party from the words, and test it in three stricter ways.

Limits

  • Only a sample of sessions, and keywords decide which speeches count. Different keywords would give different shares.
  • Counting words does not show whether a speaker is for or against something.
  • Party labels are lopsided: 385 speeches for CDU/CSU but 43 for AfD. AfD only exists from 2017.
  • Topic models need human reading. In this project, about a third of speeches ended up in BERTopic’s “no topic” bucket.
  • The notebook also pulled 30 protocols from period 20 (2021 to 2025) through the Bundestag DIP API. Those are whole protocols without party labels and are not part of the numbers on this page. The API key that was in the original notebook was removed; it should be treated as exposed and replaced.
